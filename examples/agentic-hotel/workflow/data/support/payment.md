# Payment, Deposits, and Billing Policy

This document explains payment timing, card authorization, deposits, taxes, fees, refunds, billing disputes, and common payment-related support scenarios. It is written for realistic SupportAgent answers in the Agentic Hotel example.

Support can explain payment policy and look up booking details. In this demo, support cannot charge cards, issue refunds, update payment methods, or change billing records.

## Payment Models

Hotel bookings commonly use one of several payment models.

### Pay Now

The guest pays some or all room charges when the booking is made.

Common behavior:

- The card may be charged immediately.
- Refund eligibility depends on the rate rules.
- Taxes and fees may be collected upfront or adjusted at check-in.
- The guest may still need a card at check-in for incidentals.

### Pay at Property

The guest reserves the room now and pays the hotel during the stay.

Common behavior:

- A card may be required to guarantee the booking.
- The hotel may authorize the card before arrival.
- The final charge is usually processed at check-in or check-out.

### Deposit

Some bookings require a deposit before arrival.

Common deposit types:

- First night plus tax.
- Fixed dollar amount.
- Percentage of total stay.
- Full stay for peak dates or special events.

A deposit may be refundable or non-refundable depending on the rate rules and cancellation timing.

### Prepaid Non-Refundable

Prepaid non-refundable bookings usually charge the full stay in advance. Cancellation or no-show may not produce a refund, even when the guest cancels early.

Support should avoid implying that early cancellation automatically creates refund eligibility for prepaid non-refundable rates.

## Card Authorization vs Charge

An authorization hold checks that funds are available. A charge captures funds.

Authorization hold:

- May appear as pending on the guest's account.
- May reduce available credit temporarily.
- Usually expires or is released after check-out if unused.
- Is controlled partly by the issuing bank.

Charge:

- Is a completed transaction.
- Appears in posted transactions.
- Requires a refund or reversal if it needs to be returned.

Support should ask whether the guest sees a pending authorization or a posted charge before explaining next steps.

## Incidentals

Incidentals are potential charges beyond the room rate.

Examples:

- Room service.
- Parking.
- Minibar or marketplace items.
- Resort or destination fees.
- Damage or smoking fees.
- Extra cleaning fees.
- Late check-out fees.

Hotels commonly authorize an incidentals amount at check-in. The unused portion is released after departure, but release timing depends on the bank.

## Taxes and Fees

The quoted room price may not include all taxes and fees.

Possible charges:

- Occupancy tax.
- Tourism assessment.
- Destination fee.
- Resort fee.
- Parking tax.
- Local regulatory fees.
- Service charges for packages or amenities.

Some fees are mandatory and charged by the hotel even if the guest does not use every amenity. Others are optional add-ons.

Support should distinguish between room rate, taxes, mandatory fees, optional services, and incidental charges.

## Currency and Exchange Rates

Bookings may be displayed in one currency and charged in another, depending on the guest's card, bank, and hotel location.

Important details:

- The guest's bank may apply an exchange rate.
- Foreign transaction fees may be charged by the bank.
- Refunds may differ slightly from original charges because exchange rates change.
- The hotel usually cannot control bank conversion fees.

Support should not promise exact currency conversion outcomes.

## Failed Payments

A payment can fail for many reasons.

Common causes:

- Insufficient funds or credit limit.
- Bank fraud prevention.
- Incorrect card number, expiration date, or security code.
- Card not enabled for travel or online transactions.
- Billing address mismatch.
- Expired virtual card.

Possible consequences:

- Booking may remain pending until payment succeeds.
- Hotel may request a new payment method.
- Guaranteed hold may be lost if payment is not resolved by the deadline.
- Promotional rate may no longer be available after failed payment.

## Split Payments

Guests may ask to split payment across cards, people, or payment methods.

Typical rules:

- Online booking systems may not support split payment.
- The hotel may allow split payment at check-out.
- Deposits and prepaid rates usually require one payment method.
- Refunds normally return to the original payment method, not a different card.

Support should avoid promising split payment unless the hotel has confirmed it.

## Corporate Billing

Corporate billing may involve direct bill accounts, virtual cards, travel agency billing, or negotiated invoices.

Common rules:

- The booking must be associated with an approved corporate account.
- The guest may still need a card for incidentals.
- Tax-exempt status may require documentation before check-out.
- Some charges, such as parking or minibar, may not be covered by the corporate account.

Support should tell guests to verify what the corporate billing arrangement covers.

## Refunds

Refunds depend on rate rules, cancellation timing, and whether the charge has posted.

Refund process:

- Determine whether the guest is eligible.
- Confirm whether the amount is a charge or pending authorization.
- Refund approved posted charges to the original payment method.
- Allow processing time by the hotel/platform and issuing bank.

Typical timing:

- Processing by the hotel or platform may take 3 to 7 business days.
- Bank posting may take 2 to 10 additional business days.
- International, prepaid, expired, or closed cards may take longer.

Support should not guarantee an exact posting date.

## Charge Disputes

Guests may dispute charges they do not recognize.

Support should gather context:

- Is it pending or posted?
- What is the transaction date?
- Does the amount match room, tax, deposit, incidental hold, or cancellation fee?
- Did the guest check out recently?
- Did the hotel assess damage, smoking, parking, or late check-out fees?

If the guest disputes a posted charge, the hotel may need to provide a folio or receipt. The guest may also contact the card issuer, but chargebacks can take time and may require documentation.

## Fraud and Risk Checks

Hotels and payment processors may review bookings for risk.

Possible triggers:

- Cardholder name differs from guest name.
- Multiple bookings with the same card.
- High-value stay.
- Last-minute booking.
- Failed payment attempts.
- Unusual billing country or IP location.

The hotel may request ID, card verification, or a signed authorization form. Failure to complete verification may affect check-in.

## Receipts and Folios

A receipt confirms payment. A folio itemizes room, tax, fees, and incidental charges.

Guests may need a folio for:

- Expense reimbursement.
- Corporate travel reporting.
- Tax records.
- Charge dispute review.

If the booking was paid through a third party, the hotel may only provide a folio for charges paid directly at the property. The third party may provide the prepaid room receipt.

## What Support Should Not Promise

Support should not promise:

- A refund when rate rules do not allow one.
- A specific bank posting date.
- That an authorization hold can be manually removed immediately.
- That the hotel will accept a card not belonging to the guest.
- That split payment is available.
- That this demo support agent can process payment changes.

Support may say:

- "That sounds like an authorization hold rather than a posted charge."
- "Refunds normally return to the original payment method."
- "The hotel may still require a card for incidentals."
- "I can explain the payment policy and look up booking details, but I cannot process payment changes from here."

## Example Scenarios

### Guest Sees Two Amounts

One amount may be a room charge and the other may be an incidentals authorization. The pending authorization may disappear after check-out if unused.

### Prepaid Booking Still Requires Card

The room may be prepaid, but the hotel can still require a card for incidentals, damage, parking, or optional purchases.

### Refund Is Approved But Not Visible

The refund may have been processed by the hotel but not yet posted by the bank. The guest should allow business-day processing time.

### Cardholder Is Not Traveling

The hotel may require a third-party authorization form or may ask the arriving guest for their own card at check-in.

### Guest Wants Refund to New Card

Refunds normally go back to the original payment method. If that account is closed, the issuing bank usually routes the credit according to its own process.
