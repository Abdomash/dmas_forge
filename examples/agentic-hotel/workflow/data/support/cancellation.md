# Cancellation Policy

This policy describes how cancellations, no-shows, and refund expectations work for Agentic Hotel bookings. It is written for support conversations and should be interpreted together with the guest's booking details when a booking ID is available.

Support can explain the policy and look up booking details. In this demo, support cannot cancel, modify, refund, or otherwise change a booking directly because SupportAgent has read-only tools only.

## Rate Categories

Each booking is associated with one of the following conceptual rate categories. The current demo rate data is simple, but support answers should use these categories when explaining realistic hotel policy behavior.

### Flexible Rate

Flexible rates are designed for guests whose plans may change. They normally allow cancellation without penalty until the property's free-cancellation deadline.

Typical rule:

- Free cancellation until 6:00 PM local hotel time, one calendar day before check-in.
- After the deadline, the first night room rate plus applicable taxes may be charged.
- If a booking includes multiple rooms, the late-cancellation fee may apply per room.

Example: A guest checking in Friday may cancel without penalty until Thursday at 6:00 PM local hotel time. If they cancel Friday morning, the hotel may charge one night plus tax.

### Semi-Flexible Rate

Semi-flexible rates have a lower price than flexible rates and a stricter deadline.

Typical rule:

- Free cancellation until 72 hours before check-in.
- Cancellation inside 72 hours may incur a fee equal to one night plus tax.
- Changes may be treated like cancellation and rebooking if the new stay dates are not available.

Example: A guest checking in Friday at 3:00 PM should cancel by Tuesday at 3:00 PM local hotel time to avoid the penalty.

### Advance Purchase Rate

Advance purchase rates are usually discounted because the guest commits early.

Typical rule:

- Non-refundable after booking confirmation.
- Full prepayment may be collected shortly after booking.
- The hotel may deny cancellation refunds even if the guest cancels weeks before arrival.

Support should avoid saying that a refund is guaranteed for an advance purchase booking. The best answer is that the rate is normally non-refundable and any exception would require hotel approval.

### Promotional Rate

Promotional rates may have special rules tied to a campaign, loyalty offer, or limited sale.

Typical rule:

- Cancellation terms vary by promotion.
- Some promotions are fully non-refundable.
- Some allow cancellation but do not restore the promotional price if the guest later rebooks.
- Add-ons included in the promotion, such as breakfast or parking, may not be refundable separately.

### Group or Corporate Rate

Group and corporate bookings may be controlled by a contract rather than the standard hotel policy.

Typical rule:

- Cancellation deadlines may be earlier than public rates.
- The group organizer or corporate travel manager may need to approve changes.
- Attrition, room block, or negotiated billing terms may apply.

Support should tell the guest that group and corporate bookings can have contract-specific rules.

## Cancellation Windows

The cancellation window is measured using the hotel's local time zone, not the guest's home time zone. If a guest is traveling internationally, support should mention that the hotel's local time controls the deadline.

### Free-Cancellation Window

A booking cancelled before the deadline should not incur a cancellation penalty, unless the rate is non-refundable by design. Any temporary authorization hold may still take several business days to disappear from the guest's card.

### Late Cancellation

A cancellation after the deadline may be charged. The most common late-cancellation fee is one night plus taxes, but the exact amount depends on rate type, property rules, room count, and length of stay.

### Same-Day Cancellation

Same-day cancellation is usually treated as late cancellation. If the guest cancels after the hotel's local check-in time, the hotel may treat the booking as a no-show instead.

### No-Show

A no-show occurs when the guest does not arrive and does not cancel before the no-show cutoff. The no-show cutoff is commonly overnight after the check-in date, but some properties hold rooms only until a stated time unless the guest contacts the hotel.

Typical no-show consequences:

- The first night plus tax may be charged for flexible or semi-flexible rates.
- The full stay may remain non-refundable for advance purchase rates.
- Remaining nights may be cancelled by the property if the guest does not contact the hotel.

## Refunds

Refund eligibility and refund timing are separate issues. A guest may be eligible for a refund, but the money may not appear immediately.

### Refund Destination

Refunds normally return to the original payment method. Support should not promise that funds can be redirected to a different card, bank account, or person.

### Refund Timing

Common timing expectations:

- The hotel or platform may process an approved refund within 3 to 7 business days.
- The issuing bank may take an additional 2 to 10 business days to post the credit.
- International cards, prepaid cards, and closed accounts can take longer.

Support should phrase timing as an estimate, not a guarantee.

### Authorization Holds

A pending authorization is not the same as a completed charge. If a guest sees a pending amount after cancellation, it may expire automatically. The guest's bank controls when pending holds disappear.

## Partial Cancellations

Partial cancellation means cancelling part of a booking rather than the entire stay.

Examples:

- Cancelling one room from a two-room booking.
- Cancelling the final night of a three-night stay.
- Cancelling one guest package or add-on.

Partial cancellations can be complicated because the booking may have been priced as a package. Removing one night or one room may cause repricing, loss of a discount, or minimum-stay violations.

Support should explain that partial cancellation is subject to rate rules and hotel approval. In this demo, support can explain the policy but cannot perform the change.

## Shortened Stays

If a guest checks in and later wants to leave early, the request is not always treated like a normal cancellation.

Typical outcomes:

- Flexible rates may refund unused nights if the hotel is informed before the next night's cutoff.
- Non-refundable rates may not refund unused nights.
- Some hotels charge an early departure fee.
- Package inclusions may not be refundable after check-in.

Example: A guest booked four nights, stayed two, and wants to leave because plans changed. A flexible booking may allow a refund for nights three and four if the request is made on time. An advance purchase booking may not.

## Duplicate Bookings

A duplicate booking means the same guest accidentally booked overlapping stays at the same or similar hotel.

Support should look for:

- Same guest name.
- Same hotel.
- Same or overlapping dates.
- Same card or contact details.
- Booking timestamps close together.

If the duplicate appears accidental, hotels often review it sympathetically, but support should not guarantee fee waivers. The guest may still need hotel approval, especially for non-refundable rates.

## Travel Disruptions and Emergencies

Hotels may consider exceptions for serious disruptions, but exceptions are discretionary.

Examples that may qualify for review:

- Flight cancellation or airport closure.
- Severe weather affecting travel to the city.
- Medical emergency.
- Government travel restriction.
- Natural disaster near the hotel or guest's departure point.

Support should ask the guest to provide documentation if the hotel requests it. A support answer should say that documentation can support an exception request, not that it guarantees a refund.

## Property-Caused Cancellations

If the hotel cannot honor a confirmed booking because of overbooking, closure, maintenance, or safety issues, the guest should generally not be penalized.

Possible remedies:

- Full refund of prepaid amounts.
- Relocation to a comparable nearby hotel.
- Waiver of cancellation or no-show fees.
- Assistance from hotel management.

Support should distinguish this from guest-initiated cancellation. If the property caused the issue, the guest's remedies may be stronger.

## What Support Should Not Promise

Support should not promise:

- A guaranteed refund when the rate is non-refundable.
- A guaranteed fee waiver for late cancellation.
- A specific bank posting date.
- Cancellation completion in this demo environment.
- Changes to a booking without an appropriate booking-management tool.

Support may say:

- "Based on the policy, this appears eligible for review."
- "The hotel may charge the first night plus tax."
- "Refund timing depends on both processing and the issuing bank."
- "I can explain the policy and look up booking details, but I cannot cancel the booking from here."

## Example Scenarios

### Flexible Booking Cancelled Two Days Early

The guest booked one room on a flexible rate and cancels two days before check-in. The cancellation is normally free. If there was a card authorization, it may remain pending temporarily.

### Flexible Booking Cancelled After Check-In Time

The guest cancels at 8:00 PM on the arrival date. This is usually late cancellation or no-show territory. The hotel may charge the first night plus tax.

### Advance Purchase Booking Cancelled One Month Early

Even though the cancellation is early, the booking may still be non-refundable because the rate type controls refund eligibility.

### Flight Cancellation on Arrival Day

The guest's flight is cancelled and they cannot arrive. The standard policy may still allow a fee, but the hotel may review documentation and waive or reduce the penalty.

### Two-Room Booking, One Room Cancelled

If the guest cancels one room but keeps another, the cancelled room may be subject to its own rate rules. Any multi-room discount may be recalculated.
