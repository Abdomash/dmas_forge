# Booking Changes Policy

This document explains how changes to hotel bookings are normally handled. It covers date changes, room count changes, guest details, special requests, shortening or extending stays, and repricing. It is designed for realistic support answers in the Agentic Hotel example.

Support can explain change rules and look up booking details. In this demo, support cannot actually modify a booking because SupportAgent has read-only tools only.

## Change vs Correction

A correction fixes a mistake without materially changing the stay. A change alters the commercial terms of the booking.

Examples of corrections:

- Fixing a misspelled guest name.
- Updating phone number or email.
- Adding an arrival note.
- Correcting capitalization.

Examples of changes:

- Changing check-in or check-out date.
- Adding or removing nights.
- Changing room count.
- Changing room type.
- Changing the primary guest to a different person.
- Adding a package or paid amenity.

Corrections are usually easier. Changes may require availability, repricing, and rate-rule review.

## Date Changes

Date changes are among the most complex booking changes because hotel availability and rates vary by night.

Common rules:

- New dates must be available.
- The booking may be repriced using current rates.
- The original rate may not be preserved.
- Minimum-stay or maximum-stay rules may apply.
- A change inside the cancellation window may be treated like cancellation and rebooking.

Example: A guest booked Friday to Sunday and wants to move to Saturday to Monday. The hotel must have availability for the new nights. If Saturday is more expensive, the guest may owe the difference.

## Extending a Stay

Extending means adding nights before or after the original booking.

Considerations:

- The same room type may not be available for the added nights.
- The guest may need to change rooms mid-stay.
- Added nights may price at a different rate.
- The extension may create a minimum-stay or package conflict.
- Payment authorization may need to increase.

Example: A guest booked one night and wants to stay two more. The hotel may have rooms available, but the original discounted rate may not apply to the extra nights.

## Shortening a Stay

Shortening means removing one or more nights from a booking.

Possible outcomes:

- Flexible bookings may allow unused nights to be removed before the cancellation deadline.
- Non-refundable bookings may not refund removed nights.
- A booking that originally met a minimum-stay rule may become invalid or be repriced.
- Packages may lose included benefits if the stay length changes.

Example: A three-night promotional booking required a minimum of three nights. If the guest removes one night, the promotion may no longer apply and the remaining nights may reprice.

## Room Count Changes

Adding rooms requires availability. Removing rooms may be treated as partial cancellation.

Adding rooms:

- Additional rooms may have a different rate.
- The hotel may not be able to place rooms near each other.
- Connecting rooms are usually requests.
- Multiple rooms may require additional guest names.

Removing rooms:

- Each removed room follows its rate's cancellation rules.
- Multi-room discounts may be recalculated.
- Group booking rules may apply if the original booking was part of a block.

Example: A guest booked three rooms and wants to remove one room the day before arrival. The removed room may incur a late-cancellation fee even though the other two rooms remain active.

## Room Type Changes

Changing room type is subject to availability and price difference.

Upgrades:

- Usually require the guest to pay the rate difference.
- May be unavailable on sold-out dates.
- May change taxes and fees.

Downgrades:

- May not produce a refund on non-refundable rates.
- May cause package benefits to change.
- May require hotel approval if the original room was guaranteed as part of a group or corporate contract.

## Guest Name Changes

Minor name corrections are normally allowed. Full guest substitution may be restricted.

Common considerations:

- The primary guest must meet age and ID requirements.
- The name at check-in should match government ID.
- Corporate or group bookings may require travel manager approval.
- Fraud checks may apply if the payer and guest are different.

Support should distinguish "fixing a typo" from "transferring the booking to someone else."

## Contact Information Changes

Updating email, phone, or arrival notes is usually low risk. However, changing contact information does not change the payment method or primary guest identity.

Support should remind guests that important hotel messages, arrival instructions, and receipts may be sent to the email address on the booking.

## Special Requests

Special requests can be added to a booking note, but many are not guaranteed.

Common special requests:

- High floor.
- Quiet room.
- Crib or rollaway bed.
- Connecting rooms.
- Allergy-friendly room.
- Early check-in.
- Late check-out.
- Accessible features.

Support should use careful wording: "The hotel can note the request" rather than "The request is confirmed," unless the booking specifically confirms it.

## Rate Recalculation

When a booking changes, the price may be recalculated. The new price can be higher or lower than the original price.

Reasons for recalculation:

- Different dates.
- Different room type.
- Different occupancy.
- Changed tax or fee structure.
- Promotion no longer available.
- Minimum-stay rule no longer met.

The original rate is not always portable to new dates or room types.

## Same-Day Changes

Same-day changes are often the hardest to approve.

Possible constraints:

- Housekeeping has already prepared rooms.
- Cancellation deadline has passed.
- The hotel may be sold out.
- Payment authorization may already be captured.
- No-show or early departure rules may apply.

Support should avoid promising same-day changes. The best answer is that same-day requests require property approval.

## Split Stays

A split stay occurs when the guest keeps some nights but changes room type, hotel, or booking details for other nights.

Examples:

- First night in a king room, second night in a double room.
- Two separate reservations for consecutive nights.
- Moving rooms mid-stay due to availability.

Split stays can solve availability problems but may require the guest to check out and check back in, move luggage, and update payment authorization.

## Package and Add-On Changes

Packages can include breakfast, parking, credits, late check-out, or attraction tickets. Changing dates or room type can affect package eligibility.

Rules:

- Unused package inclusions may not be refundable.
- Add-ons may be limited by date or inventory.
- Removing a package may cause the base room rate to reprice.

## What Support Should Not Promise

Support should not promise:

- That a change can be made without availability.
- That the original rate will be preserved.
- That a non-refundable booking can be changed for free.
- That special requests are guaranteed.
- That this demo support agent can perform the change.

Support may say:

- "Changes are subject to availability and rate rules."
- "The hotel may need to reprice the booking."
- "This appears to be a correction rather than a commercial change."
- "I can explain the policy and look up the booking, but I cannot modify it from here."

## Example Scenarios

### Move Booking One Week Later

The guest wants the same room next weekend. The hotel must have availability for the new dates. The rate may change because weekend demand may differ.

### Add One Room

The guest has one room and wants a second. The new room is priced at the current available rate and may not be adjacent to the first room.

### Remove Final Night

If the rate is flexible and the deadline has not passed, the removed night may be refundable. If the stay was a minimum-stay promotion, repricing may apply.

### Correct Guest Name Typo

Changing "Jon Smith" to "John Smith" is usually a correction. Replacing "John Smith" with "Maria Garcia" may be a guest substitution and may require approval.

### Request High Floor

The hotel can add the request, but it depends on availability at check-in and is not guaranteed unless specifically confirmed.
